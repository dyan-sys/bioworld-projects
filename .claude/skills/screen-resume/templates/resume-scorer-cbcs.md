## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This document provides detailed instructions for AI agents to screen **Customer Support Specialist** candidates for Care & Bloom (Hello Nancy), a women-led sexual wellness and intimate health brand scaling across the US, UK, and EU. The role splits **70–80% daily ticket support** with **20–30% project work** (SOPs, root-cause analysis, reporting, process improvement).

**This is not a traditional ticket-only role.** The ideal candidate is a problem-solver who spots patterns, fixes root causes, and improves systems — not just someone who closes tickets. They need judgment in ambiguous situations, empathetic written communication, and the initiative to drive improvements without being told.

**CRITICAL EXPERIENCE REQUIREMENT:** Candidates must have 2+ years of customer service experience, ideally in D2C, e-commerce, or consumer wellness. Candidates with no CS experience receive "DO NOT PROCEED."

**CRITICAL AGE REQUIREMENT:** All candidates estimated to be >36 years old (as of 2026) must have their recommendation capped at "PROCEED WITH CAUTION" regardless of other strengths. Age must be estimated from graduation dates or stated age, and the override must be explicitly mentioned in the rationale.

**SENSITIVITY NOTE:** This role involves conversations about sexual wellness, intimacy, and menopause. Candidates who have worked in wellness, health, personal care, or similarly sensitive industries get bonus context points — but sensitivity comfort is best assessed in interviews, not resumes.

## BUCKET 3: SKILLS (25% weight)

### Purpose
Assess the candidate's core competencies for this role. **This is not a traditional CS skills assessment.** The three most important signals are:

1. **Initiative & Ownership** — Do they drive improvements without being told?
2. **Problem Solving & Root-Cause Thinking** — Do they fix systems, not just symptoms?
3. **Results Orientation** — Do they describe work in terms of outcomes and impact, not just tasks?

These are supplemented by CS tooling proficiency, AI/automation fluency, and reporting skills.

### Scoring Breakdown

#### 3.1 Initiative & Ownership (Raw Points: 0-3)

**This is one of the most important signals for this role.** Look for evidence that the candidate drives outcomes proactively — not just executing assigned tasks.

| Score | Definition |
|-------|-----------|
| **3** | EXCEEDS: Clear evidence of proactive initiative — identified problems and fixed them without being asked; proposed and implemented process changes; took end-to-end ownership of projects or outcomes; led improvements that affected the team or business |
| **2** | ABOVE: Demonstrates ownership — took initiative on some projects; managed responsibilities beyond basic role scope; shows autonomy in decision-making |
| **1** | MEETS: Some ownership — followed processes but showed occasional initiative; executed tasks with some autonomy |
| **0** | BELOW: Purely reactive — waited for instructions; no evidence of initiative or self-direction |

**What to look for:** Phrases like "identified and implemented," "proposed a new process," "built a system for," "took ownership of," "led the effort to." Contrast with purely passive language: "was responsible for," "handled tickets," "answered calls."

#### 3.2 Problem Solving & Root-Cause Thinking (Raw Points: 0-3)

**Critical for this role.** The brand needs people who fix root causes, not just close tickets. Look for pattern recognition, analytical thinking, and systems-level problem solving.

| Score | Definition |
|-------|-----------|
| **3** | EXCEEDS: Identified patterns or trends in customer issues; investigated root causes and proposed/implemented fixes; reduced repeat issues through systemic changes; evidence of analytical thinking (e.g., "discovered 30% of returns were caused by X and fixed the packaging") |
| **2** | ABOVE: Some problem-solving evidence — troubleshot complex issues; investigated beyond surface-level symptoms; contributed to process fixes |
| **1** | MEETS: Basic troubleshooting — resolved individual issues but no evidence of pattern recognition or root-cause work |
| **0** | BELOW: No problem-solving evidence — purely transactional ticket handling |

#### 3.3 Results Orientation (Raw Points: 0-3)

**How does the candidate describe their work?** The best candidates talk about outcomes and impact — not just tasks they performed. This signals someone who understands the "why" behind their work.

| Score | Definition |

| **1** | MEETS: Task-focused with hints of outcomes — mostly describes responsibilities but occasionally mentions results |

## PART 3: RECOMMENDATION DETERMINATION

### Step 1: Calculate Final Score

Calculate the final score (0-100) using the weighted bucket formula from Part 1.

### Step 2: Determine Base Recommendation

Use the threshold assessment table (from Part 1) to determine the base recommendation based on:
1. Final Score (0-100)
2. Number of thresholds passed (0/3, 1/3, 2/3, or 3/3)

### Step 3: Apply Age Override (CRITICAL)

**Age Override Rule:**

If the candidate's estimated age > 36 years (as of 2026), apply the following override:

```
IF Estimated Age > 36:
  IF Base Recommendation is "STRONG PROCEED" OR "PROCEED" OR "PROCEED WITH QUESTIONS":
    → DOWNGRADE to "PROCEED WITH CAUTION"
    → MUST explicitly state age override in rationale

  IF Base Recommendation is "PROCEED WITH CAUTION" OR "DO NOT PROCEED":
    → KEEP original recommendation (already appropriately cautious)
    → MUST mention age factor in rationale
```

**Rationale Requirement:**

When age override applies, the "key_gaps" section MUST include:
- Estimated age calculation (show graduation year → estimated age)
- Explicit statement: "Age override applied: Estimated age [X] exceeds 36-year threshold. Recommendation capped at PROCEED WITH CAUTION."

**No Age Information Available:**

If you cannot estimate age from any available information:
- Note in rationale: "Unable to estimate age — no graduation date or age information provided."
- Do NOT apply age override
- Proceed with base recommendation

### Step 4: Apply Mandatory Requirement Check

**Insufficient CS Experience (<2 years):**
- IF section 2.1 (Total CS/CX Experience) = 0 points (no CS-titled roles at all)
- → Final Recommendation = "DO NOT PROCEED"
- → Rationale must state: "Candidate has no customer service experience (minimum 2 years required)"

### Step 5: Determine Routing Suggestion

| Condition | Suggested Route |
|-----------|----------------|
| Score ≥ 65 AND all 3 thresholds passed | **Live Interview** |
| Score 50-64, OR score ≥ 65 with 1+ threshold gap | **Async Assessment** |
| Score < 50 or DO NOT PROCEED recommendation | **—** (no route) |

### Step 6: Finalize Recommendation

The final recommendation after all overrides and checks becomes the official recommendation in the JSON output.

## EVALUATION PRINCIPLES

- **Initiative is king.** A candidate who proactively improved processes at a small company is more valuable than a passive ticket-handler at a Fortune 500. Weight what they *did* over where they *worked*.
- **Results over responsibilities.** "Reduced response time by 40%" tells you more than "Managed customer inquiries." Look for outcomes, metrics, and impact language.
- **AI fluency is a real differentiator.** The brand uses AI in support workflows. Candidates who already work with AI CS tools (Siena, Ada, etc.) or use AI for productivity (ChatGPT for drafting, automation) will ramp faster.
- **Sensitivity is tested in interviews, not resumes.** Few resumes will say "comfortable discussing sexual wellness." Wellness/health industry experience is a positive signal, but don't penalize candidates without it — comfort with sensitive topics is best assessed in R1/R2.
- **Project work signals separate good from great.** The 20-30% project work split is what makes this role special. Candidates who show SOPs, reporting, root-cause analysis, or process improvement evidence are the ones who will thrive.
- **Target calibration:** ~75-80 for a Customer Support Specialist with 3+ years at a D2C brand, helpdesk proficiency, evidence of initiative and root-cause work, and some reporting/AI exposure.
