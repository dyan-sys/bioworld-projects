# Client Meeting Prep

Patterns for preparing client-facing meetings (deep-dives, exploration calls, working sessions).

## Pull all context first

Gather everything available before touching the deck:
- Existing presentation deck
- Notion notes from prior calls (exploration, check-ins)
- JDs, briefs, or docs the client has shared
- Any Slack/email threads with context

Don't prep blind. The quality of the prep is proportional to the context you absorb.

## The critical question: "Does this show we understand them?"

A template deck signals we didn't listen. Client-specific content signals we did.

Before every meeting, ask: "If the client saw this deck cold, would they feel understood — or would they feel like we're running a generic playbook on them?"

## Don't make the client repeat themselves

If they told you something in a prior call, reflect it back — not as a question, but as confirmation. Use their own words where possible (direct quotes land well).

Replace generic discovery slides with "What We Heard" — invert the energy from "tell us about yourself" to "here's what we understand, correct us."

## Pre-populate frameworks

Work audits, discovery grids, delegation matrices, etc. should arrive with what we already know filled in. The meeting refines and prioritizes — it doesn't discover from zero.

If the client has already shared a JD or task list, map it into the framework beforehand and acknowledge their effort.

## Surface tensions proactively

Flag scope issues, skill mismatches, reporting line conflicts, and automation candidates before the meeting. Frame them as "discussion points" — not problems, but decisions to make together.

Examples:
- "This JD covers marketing + ops + CS — is that one person or two?"
- "Ad kill/scale decisions require judgment — needs tight SOPs or a more senior profile"
- "Two reporting lines — how do we manage time allocation?"

These are where the real value of the session lives.

## Compress what they've already heard

Don't re-pitch to someone who's past the exploration stage. One recap slide, not five. The deep-dive should be about them, not about us.

## Update the agenda to match reality

The agenda should reflect the actual session flow, not the template's default. If you've added personalized sections, the agenda must show them. A mismatched agenda breaks trust immediately.

## Prepare a teammate handoff

After prepping, send a Slack note to the teammate running the meeting:
- Link to the deck
- What was changed and why
- Clear actions (e.g., "decide which duplicate slides to keep/delete")
- Any context they need that isn't in the slides
