# Ally LinkedIn Content Strategy

## 1. Why We Are Using LinkedIn

LinkedIn is not a growth experiment for us. It is a positioning channel.

Our **primary objective** over the next 3–6 months is **candidate attraction**.

We are optimizing for one outcome:

- Bringing in **very high‑quality Executive Assistant candidates** who are selective about where they work

The reasoning is straightforward:

- Strong candidates are the foundation of service quality
- Service quality ultimately drives sales and long‑term growth

We are not pursuing visibility for its own sake.

Our focus is on sending a clear, consistent signal about how we operate, what we value, and the standard we expect.

The intent is practical:

- Help the right candidates recognize Ally as a place they would thrive
- Help the wrong candidates self-select out

Inbound executive interest may occur over time, but it is not what we are optimizing for.

---

## 2. Who We Are Speaking To

### Primary Audience

Our primary audience is **high‑caliber Executive Assistants** who are evaluating their *next career step*, not just their next role.

At their core, these candidates are asking:

> "Is this an opportunity that meaningfully propels my career forward?"

Our content should help them assess that question clearly and honestly.

Specifically, they are looking for signal across several dimensions:

- **Clientele:** Who will I be supporting? What caliber of leaders does this company work with?
- **Scope of Work:** What type of problems will I actually be trusted to handle?
- **Learning & Development:** How will I grow my judgment, skills, and operating range over time?
- **AI & Tools:** How seriously does this company think about AI—and how it changes my role?
- **Career Trajectory:** What does progression look like here, in practice?
- **Leadership Quality:** Who are the leaders I will work with, and how do they operate?

Our role is not to sell. It is to provide enough clarity that the *right* candidates can see themselves building a meaningful career at Ally.

### Secondary Audience

- Founders and executives who value focus, leverage, and working on the right problems

While candidates are the priority, credibility with executives matters because it reinforces the quality of leaders and environments our EAs are exposed to.

The content should feel rigorous enough that candidates trust the caliber of people they will be supporting.

---

## 3. How We Want to Be Perceived

Repeated exposure to our content should leave people with a clear impression:

- This team understands executive leverage.
- They are intentional, not reactive.
- They communicate clearly and think in principles.
- I would trust them.
- I would consider working here.

The guiding principle is not perfection or being consistently right. It is that **every post contains a nugget of insight that is useful to someone**.

Some posts may be aimed primarily at Executive Partners, others at executives—but in each case, there should be **something interesting, actionable, or thought-provoking**. It doesn't have to be profound; it just has to be worth sharing and worth reading.

---

## 4. Voice & Style

### Core Characteristics

- Executive
- Calm
- Human
- Precise

Warmth matters, but clarity matters more.

### Writing Guidelines

- Lead with insight, not slogans
- Explain the reasoning behind opinions
- Prefer specificity over general productivity advice
- Write as operators, not commentators

### Avoid

- Trend‑chasing
- Overly casual language
- Generic leadership platitudes

---

## 5. Content Pillars

### Pillar 1: Delegation & Executive Leverage

Focus: how real leverage is created.

Topics: why delegation fails in practice, what should not be delegated, the difference between task support and leverage, how assistants create asymmetric impact.

### Pillar 2: Focus & Priority Management

Focus: working on the right problems.

Topics: focus as a leadership responsibility, the cost of constant context switching, decision quality vs. speed, protecting executive attention.

### Pillar 3: How We Operate at Ally

Focus: signaling standards and culture without performative transparency.

Topics: how we think about ownership, how feedback is given and received, what excellence looks like in our roles, how we balance rigor and humanity.

### Pillar 4: Tools, AI, and the Modern EA

Focus: relevance without hype.

Topics: what tools meaningfully change executive support, where AI helps and where judgment still matters, how the EA role is evolving.

### Pillar 5: Life at Ally

Focus: share stories on what EPs do for clients.

Topics: project spotlights, problem-solving in action, client impact stories, learning & development in practice, collaboration & leadership exposure, career progression stories, culture & values in action, behind-the-scenes of complex projects.

### Pillar 6: Time Management & Prioritization

Focus: how EPs protect executive time, reduce decision fatigue, and create leverage through smart prioritization.

Topics: calendar optimization, prioritization frameworks, managing competing priorities, decision load reduction, focus protection, reactive to proactive shifts, time leverage wins.

### Pillar 7: Achievements & Outcomes

Focus: highlight the tangible and intangible results EPs deliver—measured in outcomes, not activity.

Topics: outcome spotlights, before & after stories, metrics that matter, unblocking progress, execution wins, strategic impact, quiet wins, compounding impact.

---

## 6. Cadence

One post per week on the company LinkedIn. Consistency and quality take precedence over volume.

---

## 7. Post Formats

Rotate formats to maintain range:

- Short principle‑driven posts
- Clear POVs on delegation and focus
- Operational insights from behind the scenes
- Occasional hiring‑adjacent signals (without calls to action)

---

## 8. Content Creation Process

1. **Input** — Internal discussions, hiring insights, founder/team observations, real operating decisions
2. **Research** — Web search for fresh angles, data points, contrarian takes
3. **Drafting** — AI-assisted structuring and refinement with Ally voice
4. **Review** — Human judgment for clarity, tone, and accuracy (Dyan approves topics/drafts, Ivan final approves)
5. **Publish** — Observe resonance over time, not individual post performance

---

## 9. What Success Looks Like

We will know this is working when:

- Candidates reference our thinking unprompted
- The content feels cohesive quarter over quarter
- Our point of view becomes easier to articulate internally
